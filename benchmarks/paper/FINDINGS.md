# Paper benchmark: what the results say

The numbers are in `RESULTS.md` (generated by `summarize.py`, raw per-arm values in `results/`); the
design and the claim-to-benchmark map are in `README.md`. This file interprets them: what the final
method achieves, why each default is the default, and which weaknesses a reviewer will find.

- 189 of 192 jobs ran at commit 8ebb7982 (2026-10-26 to 2026-10-28, GPU 7).
- Three jobs failed on driver bugs and were rerun after harness-only fixes (`0cd9b664`, `c9a7e8c8`);
  the method code is unchanged between these commits:
  - `pp_fnpse_s7`, which now runs on the `nowide` net, see §4.7;
  - `gausstoy_cov_small`;
  - `gausstoy_cov_1000`.
- Four jobs were added on 2026-10-29: the head-to-head below (`pp_fairtest_s7/s8/s9`) and a timing
  run with the GPU to itself (`pp_fairtest_timing_s7`).
- All 196 jobs finished.

"Noise" below means the spread between training seeds of the same recipe. On partial pooling (PP),
that spread is 0.936–0.947 in mean r over 100 datasets, and 0.938–0.952 over 30.

## 1. Headline results

### Partial pooling against BayesFlow on BayesFlow's own network (`RESULTS.md` §1)

Both arms use the same upstream SetTransformer + DiffusionModel, the same simulations and the same 100
test datasets. Only the composition rule differs.

| n | prior | COMPASS mean r | BayesFlow mean r | COMPASS ECE / cov90 | BayesFlow ECE / cov90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | wide training prior | 0.926 (3 inference seeds 0.926–0.933) | 0.926 | **0.038 / 0.87** | 0.106 / 0.79 |
| 25 | wide | **0.891** | 0.872 | 0.038 / 0.91 | 0.035 / 0.92 |
| 4 | wide | **0.661** | 0.621 | **0.023 / 0.91** | 0.157 / 1.00 |
| 100 | inference prior | 0.886 | **0.899** | **0.031 / 0.89** | 0.247 / 0.57 |

- **Locals:** r 0.942 vs 0.907 at n = 100.
- **Recovery:** COMPASS ties or beats upstream in recovery everywhere except one row, the
  inference-prior net at n = 100 (−0.013 r).
- **Calibration** is the clearest win. On that same row, BayesFlow's 90% intervals cover 57% of the
  truths, COMPASS's 89%.

### Head-to-head on BayesFlow's test datasets: BayesFlow vs the full COMPASS method (`RESULTS.md` §1b)

The full COMPASS method means its own network and sampler, with the final PP recipe, on upstream
BayesFlow's 100 test datasets (converted by `fairtest.py`; same simulator, prior and trials). Every
arm is scored by the same `fair.py score`. n = 100, wide training prior everywhere.

| arm | globals mean r | NRMSE | ECE | cov90 | locals r | locals ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BayesFlow (`compositional_sample`, global + local net) | 0.926 | 0.29 | 0.106 | 0.79 | 0.907 | 0.082 |
| COMPASS rule on BayesFlow's joint net (3 inference seeds) | 0.928 (0.926–0.933) | 0.31 | **0.039** | **0.88** | 0.942 | 0.026 |
| **full COMPASS** (3 training seeds) | **0.937** (0.933–0.940) | 0.29 | 0.072 (0.059–0.086) | 0.83 | **0.943** | **0.019** |

NRMSE moves by about ±0.005 between scorings, because BayesFlow's metric resamples.

- **Recovery:** the full method beats BayesFlow on every training seed (+0.011 r on average). It
  wins on all 7 globals for seed 7, and on 6 of 7 for seeds 8 and 9. The locals are
  clearly better (r 0.943 vs 0.907; local ECE 0.019 vs 0.082).
- **Calibration:**
  - better than BayesFlow (ECE 0.072 vs 0.106);
  - worse than the COMPASS rule on BayesFlow's own network (0.039);
  - the COMPASS network trades some calibration for its extra accuracy (§4.2).
- **The two layers of the comparison:**
  - same network, only the rule changes: calibration improves, recovery does not change;
  - the COMPASS network added on top: recovery and the locals improve.

**Cost** (RTX 2080 Ti):

| | training | simulations | inference per dataset (n = 100, 100 draws) |
|---|---|---|---|
| BayesFlow | 233 min (global net) + 269 min (local net) = 8.4 h | 2 × 32,768 | 0.2 s |
| COMPASS rule on BayesFlow's joint net | 286 min (joint net) | 65,536 | 2.1 s (8 datasets per batch) |
| full COMPASS | 147–201 min (score net) + a short encoder pretraining (37 epochs) | 65,536 | **47 s with the GPU alone**; 130–200 s with 3 jobs sharing it |

- The full method is about 250× slower at inference than BayesFlow's own composition. The cost
  is in the network: a 1 M-parameter transformer over 26 tokens, roughly 50× the MLP per
  evaluation.
- The batching is worse too: one dataset and 25 draws per call, against 8 datasets and all draws
  on BayesFlow's net.
- The rule itself costs 13× over BayesFlow's plain sum on the same network (2.1 vs 0.16 s).
- Speed-ups that are measured or safe, none of them in the method yet:
  - 25 steps: no measured loss, 2×;
  - netcov instead of the FD Jacobian: 1.3–1.5×;
  - batching datasets and draws: 2–4×;
  - fp16 for the transformer: 1.5–2.5×.

  Together they could plausibly bring 47 s to a few seconds per dataset.

### The best COMPASS combination: final recipe sampled with the covariance head (netcov)

The final recipe keeps EDM, because EDM is the likelihood-accuracy choice. It is sampled with the
network covariance (netcov r9) instead of the FD Jacobian. Everything else is identical: same
networks, 3 training seeds, 100 datasets per test set.

| test set | sampler | globals r | ECE | cov90 | z_rms | locals r | time (shared GPU) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BayesFlow's | FD Jacobian | 0.937 (0.933–0.940) | 0.072 (0.059–0.086) | 0.83 | — | 0.943 | 245 min / 100 datasets |
| BayesFlow's | **netcov** | 0.937 (0.932–0.942) | **0.062** (0.043–0.077) | **0.84** | — | 0.943 | **145 min** |
| ours | FD Jacobian | 0.940 (0.936–0.947) | — | 0.82 | 1.21 | — | 127 s / dataset |
| ours | **netcov** | 0.941 (0.936–0.948) | — | **0.84** | **1.14** | — | 110 s / dataset |

- **Recovery:** identical with both samplers.
- **Calibration:** netcov is better on every seed of BayesFlow's test set:
  - ECE 0.086 → 0.077, 0.072 → 0.066 and 0.059 → 0.043;
  - z_rms on our set 1.21 → 1.14.
- **Speed:** netcov is 1.7× faster on BayesFlow's test set. It needs no extra finite-difference
  forward passes.
- **Against BayesFlow (ECE 0.106, cov90 0.79):** this combination is the best COMPASS arm,
  r 0.937 vs 0.926 and ECE 0.062 vs 0.106.
- **Against our rule on BayesFlow's own network** (ECE 0.039, cov90 0.88): this combination is
  still less calibrated. The network's per-subject bias, summed over 100 subjects (§4.2), is
  reduced but not removed.
- **FD stays the library default** because netcov is worse on IK reach (§2, last row). For
  partial pooling, netcov is the recommended sampler.

case as worse when W1 is more than 15% above the final arm's.

| period 9 | 5, 10, 19 (fair) | 0.923 / 0.933 / 0.936 vs 0.926; all inside the inference-seed spread (0.926–0.933) | accuracy is flat in the period; 9 is kept from the earlier speed study, not because this run separates it |

**BayesFlow's test set** (BayesFlow itself: r 0.926, ECE 0.106, cov90 0.79):

| arm | ECE per seed (7 / 8 / 9) | cov90 per seed | mean r | mean NRMSE | mean ECE | mean cov90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rg, FD | 0.086 / 0.072 / 0.059 | 0.80 / 0.83 / 0.85 | 0.937 | **0.29** | 0.072 | 0.83 |
| Rg, netcov | 0.077 / 0.066 / 0.043 | 0.83 / 0.83 / 0.86 | 0.937 | **0.29** | **0.062** | 0.84 |
| no Rg, FD | **0.203** / 0.054 / 0.051 | 0.65 / 0.87 / 0.86 | 0.936 | 0.32 | 0.103 | 0.79 |
| no Rg, netcov | **0.192** / 0.063 / 0.053 | 0.66 / 0.89 / 0.88 | 0.936 | 0.32 | 0.103 | 0.81 |

**What Rg does, corrected.** The earlier one-seed result ("Rg is what calibrates the globals",
coverage 0.59 without it) does not generalise.

- **Without Rg the outcome depends on the seed:**
  - seed 7 is badly overconfident (cov90 0.60–0.66, ECE about 0.2, worse than BayesFlow);
  - seeds 8 and 9 are well calibrated, with z_rms 0.94–1.04 and cov90 0.86–0.90, better than
    with Rg;
  - but seeds 8 and 9 have larger errors (RMSE 0.38–0.45 vs 0.35–0.36) and one log σ collapse
    each.
- **With Rg every seed behaves the same:**
  - RMSE 0.346–0.366, no collapses;
  - recovery r 0.936–0.948;
  - the same mild under-coverage (cov90 0.79–0.87, z_rms 1.08–1.25).
- **Mechanism:** without Rg, the network must learn the prior part of the global scores. Composition
  subtracts that part 99 times, so a small error in it is amplified. Seed to seed, that error
  sometimes shifts the centre (seed 7: overconfident) and sometimes widens or blurs it (seeds 8
  and 9: wider and less accurate).

  Rg makes the prior part exact. That removes the seed lottery and the collapses, at the cost of
  a consistent ~15% under-coverage. The under-coverage is the network's own per-subject bias,
  which Rg does not touch.
- **netcov** helps calibration in every arm (z_rms −0.04 to −0.09) but cannot replace Rg.
- **Verdict:** Rg is kept because it makes the method **reliable**: same accuracy on every seed,
  lower error, no collapses. It is not what fixes calibration. The remaining under-coverage is a
  separate problem (§4.2).

### Early stopping vs a fixed 500 epochs, as BayesFlow trains

`pp.py` stops training after 20 epochs without validation improvement; BayesFlow trains a fixed
500 epochs. The final recipe was retrained with early stopping off (`paper_e500_s7/8/9`, exactly
500 epochs, final weights; everything else identical) and evaluated on both test sets, 100
datasets each.

| seed | epochs (early stopping → fixed) | our test set: r / RMSE / cov90 | BayesFlow's test set: r / ECE / cov90 / local ECE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 500 → 500 | 0.936 / 0.366 / 0.79 → **identical** | 0.940 / 0.086 / 0.80 / 0.023 → **identical** |
| 8 | 500 → 500 | 0.947 / 0.346 / 0.82 → **identical** | 0.938 / 0.072 / 0.83 / 0.018 → **identical** |
| 9 | 440 → 500 | 0.936 / 0.364 / 0.84 → 0.939 / 0.357 / 0.80 | 0.933 / 0.059 / 0.85 / 0.016 → 0.935 / 0.078 / 0.83 / 0.034 |

- **Training is deterministic.** Seeds 7 and 8 never triggered early stopping under seeded
  validation, and their retrainings reproduce the original networks exactly, to every printed
  digit.
- **Seed 9, the only real change**, trained 60 more epochs. Its validation loss never improved after
  epoch 419, in the fixed run as well:
  - recovery improves a little (+0.002 to +0.003 r);
  - calibration gets a little worse on both test sets (cov90 −0.02 to −0.04; ECE 0.059 → 0.078;
    local ECE 0.016 → 0.034).

  Both changes are inside the seed-to-seed spread.
- **Verdict:** training a fixed 500 epochs, as BayesFlow does, does not change the network's
  performance. With seeded validation, early stopping fires rarely (1 of 3 seeds) and late
  (epoch 440). The comparison against BayesFlow is therefore not confounded by the stopping
  rule.

## 4. Weaknesses a reviewer will find, stated

1. **EDM loss weighting does not help posterior recovery on PP; it hurts slightly.**
   - Both no-EDM seeds (0.956, 0.958) beat both final seeds (0.938, 0.952), and calibration is better too.
   - EDM entered the recipe for the PF-ODE likelihood (−32..37% log p error) and for PP RMSE in the
     roadmap-2 study at an earlier commit.
   - The paper should present EDM as a likelihood choice, or drop it from the posterior recipe.
2. **Under-coverage on PP with the COMPASS network.**
   - cover90 0.81 and z_rms 1.20 for the final method, over 300 runs.
   - netcov (0.84 / 1.13), Gloeckler (0.83 / 1.03) and churn 0 (0.86 / 1.05) are better calibrated
     at equal r.
   - The training ablations without the covariance head or EDM (0.87–0.89) are too.
   - The global posteriors are about 15–20% too narrow.
3. **The bimodal GM with a Gaussian prior interface degrades with n even with oracle scores.**
   - Latent-attention GM, oracle arm: W1 0.65 → 4.48 sd from n = 8 to 64, while the plain sum stays
     at about 0.5 (§3).
   - This is the Gaussian `prior=(mean, std)` interface, not the rule.
   - With the mixture prior (§4) the cov_lo net reaches 0.87–0.95 at n = 64, against 7–8 for every
     Gaussian-prior arm. Branch masses are still off by 0.44–0.50 at n = 64.
4. **Network bias summed over many groups (AR(1) 64×64).**
   - Every GAUSS-type rule is biased by 6–23 posterior sd on the globals.
   - F-NPSE is biased by 2–25 sd and the plain sum by 14–57 sd.
   - At 16×16 the final method is biased by 0.8–2.2 sd and 1.3–1.6× too wide.
   - This is the known score-limited regime: it needs a better network, not a different rule.
5. **IK flat-global at n = 128:** W1 3.57 sd. The gate, consistent and metric variants do better
   there (1.95–2.50), but each loses elsewhere.
6. **Seeds.**
   - The BayesFlow networks have one training seed each; the inference seed is replicated instead.
   - The architecture and prior ablations `nowide` and `full` have one seed. `norg` has three (below).
   - The sampler ablations run on the seed-7 net, with the two closest PD alternatives cross-checked
     on seed 8.
7. **F-NPSE has no training-prior correction** in the library, so on PP it runs on the `nowide`
   net, paired with JAC-FD r9 on the same net:

   | | mean r | cover90 | z_rms | s/dataset |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | JAC-FD r9 | 0.944 | 0.78 | 1.29 | 99 |
   | F-NPSE | 0.904 | 0.54 | 2.68 | 312 |

   F-NPSE is worse and 3× slower.
8. **Many groups (Gaussian toy, Arruda Exp. 1, covariance-output net):**
   - error grows with J: mean error 0.08 sd at J = 10, 0.19 sd at J = 100, 1.19 sd at J = 1000;
   - JAC and netcov agree at every J, and netcov is 1.6–2× faster;
   - the width stays right (0.97–1.01).

   This is the same network-bias-summed-over-groups limit as AR(1) 64×64. The rule does not
   fix it, so applications with more than about 100 groups need a better network or
   subsampling.

## 5. What would change the recipe

- Drop EDM from the posterior-only recipe. That is two seeds' worth of evidence and should be
  confirmed on a third seed and on IK/GM before it changes the library default.
- Consider 25 steps: no measured loss anywhere it was run.
- Address the under-coverage. netcov, or churn 0 with the Jacobian, are the cheapest candidates;
  neither was tested on all problems as the default.
